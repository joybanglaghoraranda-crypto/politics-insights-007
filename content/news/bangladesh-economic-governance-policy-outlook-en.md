**DHAKA:** Sustainable economic transformation and democratic resilience rely heavily on the strength of public institutions. Across South Asia, policy researchers and governance specialists emphasize three interconnected pillars for long-term stability: transparent public finance management, independent regulatory oversight, and accessible civic institutions.

## 1. Fiscal Transparency and Public Procurement

When citizens and independent auditors can track how tax revenues are allocated and spent, public trust in governance improves measurably. Publishing disaggregated budget execution data and strengthening electronic government procurement (e-GP) frameworks reduce implementation delays and cost overruns.

## 2. Independent Regulatory Institutions

From central banking supervision to electoral administration and anti-corruption oversight, institutional credibility depends on statutory independence, merit-based appointments, and clear accountability mechanisms before parliament and the judiciary.

## 3. Data-Driven Policy Evaluation

Modern policy design increasingly relies on verified empirical data—such as household income and expenditure surveys, labor force statistics, and inflation indices—allowing policymakers to evaluate whether public programs are reaching intended beneficiaries effectively.
